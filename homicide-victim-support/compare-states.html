Compare states

Same loss, different state

Every state compensates murder victims' families and gives them rights in the criminal case, but the details vary widely. Among the eighteen states below, total compensation caps run from $15,000 to $70,000 (New York sets limits by expense type instead), funeral limits from $5,000 to $12,818, and the deadline to apply from 180 days to seven years.

Eighteen states now. More are being added.

What governs your case is the law of the state where the death happened, not where you live. If those are different, use that state's row, and that state's compensation program.

What the total cap means. The most the program will pay for one victim's death, across every claim combined, usually including the funeral. North Carolina pays the funeral on top of its cap, Michigan's cap is per claimant, and New York sets limits by expense type instead. It is not paid to each family member, and it is not a guaranteed amount: the program pays only after insurance and other sources. Some states pay certain costs, such as counseling, outside the cap. Your state guide has the details.

03 Common questions

Questions about state differences

Which states have Marsy's Law?

Of the eighteen states compared here, California (2008), Florida (2018), Georgia (2018), North Carolina (2018) and Ohio (2018) have Marsy's Law amendments in their constitutions. Alabama, Arizona, Illinois, Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, Missouri, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee and Texas have their own constitutional victims' rights amendments under other names. New York's rights are in statute. Pennsylvania voters approved Marsy's Law in 2019, but it never took effect after a 2021 court ruling, so Pennsylvania's rights come from the Crime Victims Act.

Which state pays the most to a murder victim's family?

Among the eighteen states compared here, California has the highest caps: up to $12,818 for a funeral and $70,000 in total per victim through CalVCB. New York sets limits by expense type rather than one overall cap, with loss of support limited to $30,000. Alabama, Louisiana and South Carolina have the lowest total caps, at $15,000. Every program pays only after insurance and other sources, so the real amount depends on what else covers the loss.

How long do I have to apply for crime victim compensation after a murder?

It depends on the state. South Carolina is the shortest of the eighteen compared here, at 180 days from the crime or the death, though good cause can extend it to four years. Oregon allows one year; Alabama, Arizona, Missouri, North Carolina and Tennessee two; Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, New York, Ohio and Texas three; Maryland four; Illinois, Michigan and Pennsylvania five; and California seven. Apply as early as you can, even before an arrest.

Can a murder victim's family enforce their rights in court?

In the states with a full guide here, yes, but the route differs. California and Florida let families enforce rights as a matter of right in the trial or appellate court. Ohio and Illinois set out a motion and appeal process. Georgia allows a single motion within 20 days that cannot be appealed. Texas gives standing to enforce rights but not to act as a party. North Carolina uses a motion in the criminal case, Maryland allows an appeal when a right is denied, Arizona gives standing to bring a special action, and South Carolina allows a writ of mandamus. Elsewhere, start with the prosecutor's victim advocate.
