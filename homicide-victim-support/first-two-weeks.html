The first two weeks

In the first two weeks after a murder, five things are time-sensitive: the crime victim compensation application, a written request for notice of court proceedings, custody and release notification signup, employer leave paperwork, and the wrongful death deadline. Everything else can wait. Start by getting the case number, the detective's direct line, and the victim advocate's name.

Almost nothing on this page is urgent in the way it will feel. A handful of items are time-sensitive, and some have legal deadlines behind them, and the rest is here so that no one has to remember it while trying to plan a funeral.

Hand a whole section to someone who asked how they could help. Most of these are phone calls, and a friend can make a phone call.

By now the phone has gone quiet. This is the week to set up the things that keep information coming to you.

04 Before you make any call

Have in hand

The case number, the name of the last person you spoke to, and the date of that call. That is usually enough to get past the front desk.

Ask for, every time

Their full name, their title, a direct number, and the next step with an actual date attached to it. Vague answers are how cases go quiet.

Write down after

What they said, what they promised, and the date you will call back if nothing happens. Put that date somewhere it will find you.

05 What you do not have to do in these two weeks

Thank anybody

Not for the flowers, not for the food, not for coming. Nobody worth keeping is counting, and the ones who are counting can wait.

Clear out their things

Their room, their closet, their phone, their car. There is no deadline on any of it, whatever a relative tells you.

Decide about a lawsuit

Ask an attorney about the deadline so you know how much time you have. Knowing the date is not the same as deciding.

Forgive anyone

Not the person who did this, not the detective who was short with you, not the relative who said the wrong thing. That is not a two-week task.

And you do not have to be back at work in two weeks because that is what the policy says. Ask what leave you actually have before you agree to a date.

06 Common questions

Questions about the first two weeks

How long before the body is released after a murder?

It depends on the medical examiner or coroner. An autopsy is generally required in a homicide, and the body is not released until it is complete. Call the office yourself and ask for the expected timeline, who signs the release, and which funeral home they need named.

How many death certificates do I need?

Order ten to fifteen certified copies to start. Banks, insurers, the DMV, employers and creditors each want their own. Ask whether the cause of death is listed as pending, because a pending certificate can stall insurance and bank claims for weeks.

Do I have to go back to work right away?

Not because a policy says so. Ask HR to put your options in writing: bereavement leave, the employee assistance program, and FMLA eligibility, which does not cover grief itself but can cover a serious health condition such as trauma or depression. Some states also protect time off to attend court. HR will rarely volunteer any of it, so ask what leave you actually have before you agree to a return date.

Should I talk to a lawyer after a family member is murdered?

Yes, once, and early. A wrongful death claim runs on its own deadline, and that clock can expire while the criminal case is still going. Knowing the date is not the same as deciding to sue. Separately, nonprofits represent families at no cost to enforce victims' rights, and the National Crime Victim Law Institute can refer you. How a wrongful death claim differs from the criminal case

Should I apply for compensation if my paperwork is incomplete?

Yes. File the crime victim compensation application even if something is missing, and ask what can be supplemented later. A filed claim with gaps beats a complete claim filed after the deadline. Deadlines vary by state and some are short, so ask the victim advocate for yours.
