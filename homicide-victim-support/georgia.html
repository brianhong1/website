State guide

Your rights in Georgia

Families of murder victims in Georgia have constitutional rights under Marsy's Law, Article I, Section I, Paragraph XXX, and the Crime Victims' Bill of Rights: notice of court proceedings and of the accused's arrest or release, not being excluded from court, being heard at release, plea and sentencing, and objecting to parole. If a right is denied, you have 20 days to file a motion.

Marsy's Law, approved November 6, 2018, effective January 1, 2019

Georgia voters added victims' rights to the constitution in 2018. The law that came with it gives families one way to enforce them, with a short deadline. Here is what that means for a homicide victim's family, in plain words.

01

What you are entitled to

Georgia names one family member as the victim, in order: the spouse, or if none, an adult child, then a parent, then a sibling, then a grandparent (O.C.G.A. 17-17-3). Many rights depend on a request, so the named person should ask the prosecutor in writing and keep the copy. The others can still attend court and speak at sentencing.

  • 01 To be treated fairly and with dignity by every criminal justice agency involved in the case Paragraph XXX(a) and O.C.G.A. 17-17-1.
  • 02 On request, to reasonable, accurate and timely notice of scheduled court proceedings and any changes Paragraph XXX(a)(1). Keep the investigating agency, the prosecutor and later the Parole Board updated with your address and phone. Georgia keeps that contact information out of public records (17-17-14).
  • 03 On request, to notice of the arrest, release or escape of the accused Paragraph XXX(a)(2).
  • 04 Not to be excluded from any scheduled court proceeding, except as the law provides Paragraph XXX(a)(3). Under O.C.G.A. 17-17-9, a victim or immediate family member cannot be excluded just because they are subpoenaed to testify, unless they are a material and necessary witness and the court finds a substantial probability their presence would impair a fair trial.
  • 05 On request, to be heard at any scheduled proceeding involving release, plea or sentencing Paragraph XXX(a)(4). See the note on sentencing below before you prepare a victim impact statement.
  • 06 To give the prosecutor your opinion on plea or sentence negotiations O.C.G.A. 17-17-11 requires the prosecutor to offer you that opportunity. You are consulted, not given a veto.
  • 07 To refuse an interview with the defense, or to set your own conditions O.C.G.A. 17-17-8.1. You choose the time, place, who attends and whether it is recorded, and you can end it at any time. Defense contacts must say they are calling on behalf of the accused, and if you say no, they must stop.
  • 08 To be told of motions for new trial and appeals, on written request O.C.G.A. 17-17-12. In a death penalty case, the Attorney General also reports on collateral attacks on written request.
  • 09 To block mail, email and texts from the inmate to you and your household O.C.G.A. 17-17-12.1. The prosecutor must give you the instructions after conviction. Ask for them if you did not receive them.
  • 10 To restitution as provided by law, and to proceedings free from unreasonable delay O.C.G.A. 17-17-1. Keep every receipt from the first week on.
02 When a right is ignored

Georgia gives victims one way to enforce these rights, and a short clock. Under O.C.G.A. 17-17-15(c), as rewritten for Marsy's Law, if you made a written request to be notified or heard and were not, or any other provision was not followed, you may file a motion in the criminal case asking to be heard. It must be filed as soon as possible and no later than 20 days after the denial. That motion is the only way to enforce these rights, and the judge's decision on it is final and cannot be appealed.

1

Make the written requests early

Most of the enforcement right depends on having asked in writing first. On day one, send the prosecutor a written request to be notified of all proceedings and to be heard, with your contact information. Keep the copy.

2

Count the 20 days

If you learn a hearing happened without notice, or you were not allowed to speak, file the motion right away. Serve a copy and the hearing notice on the prosecutor and the defendant. The court may rule on paper or hold a hearing.

3

If you want legal help

You do not need a lawyer. The statute gives the right to file the motion to you, and you can file it yourself in the criminal case. If the motion is about the prosecutor's own failure, or you would rather not draft it under a 20-day deadline, a private attorney is an option. The Prosecuting Attorneys' Council lists the rights and statewide contacts. Who to call is below.

Speaking at sentencing in Georgia

Georgia treats victim impact as testimony. Under O.C.G.A. 17-10-1.2, before sentencing the court must allow the victim, the family, or other witnesses to testify about the crime's impact, in the defendant's presence, and that testimony is subject to cross-examination. If the court finds a witness cannot testify in person without undue emotion or severe distress, a written or recorded statement can be used, but the witness is still subject to cross-examination. If the prosecutor did not make a reasonable attempt to notify you, the court must pause the presentence hearing so you can attend. Prepare with the advocate, and ask what cross-examination could cover.

03 Compensation in Georgia

The program

The Georgia Crime Victims Compensation Program is administered by the Criminal Justice Coordinating Council under O.C.G.A. 17-15. For a homicide it can help with funeral costs, counseling for the victim's spouse, parents, step-parents, children, step-children, siblings and step-siblings, loss of support, and crime scene sanitization.

File within three years of the crime or of the death; the board can extend that for good cause (O.C.G.A. 17-15-5). The crime must have been reported within 72 hours, and you must pursue restitution against the offender unless the board finds that is not feasible (17-15-8).

For crimes since July 1, 2002, awards are capped at $25,000 in total. The category limits include $6,000 for funeral expenses, $3,000 for counseling for each eligible family member, $10,000 for loss of support and $1,500 for crime scene sanitization (17-15-8).

Before you file

Gather the itemized funeral statement, counseling invoices, proof of the victim's income if anyone depended on it, and anything already paid by insurance or a fundraiser. An undisclosed payment is a common reason a claim is reduced. The victim advocate in the district attorney's office can help you apply.

File even if something is missing, and ask what can be supplemented afterward. A filed claim with gaps beats a complete claim filed past the deadline.

The first two weeks, including what to keep

A separate deadline: wrongful death. A civil lawsuit runs on its own clock, apart from compensation and the criminal case. In Georgia that is two years from the death, and the criminal case does not pause it: Georgia's tolling rule for crime victims has been held not to cover a family's wrongful death claim. 04 If the case is unsolved

Georgia has no statute of limitations on murder. Under O.C.G.A. 17-3-1(a), a prosecution for murder may be commenced at any time, so the case does not expire.

1

Keep one named contact

Get the detective's name, direct number, email and the case number in writing. When that detective retires or moves units, ask who owns the case now and write it down. Send every tip you hear to that person, in writing, so it lands in the file.

2

Keep your address current

Under O.C.G.A. 17-17-14, keeping the investigating agency and prosecutor informed of your current address and phone is your responsibility. If an arrest comes years from now, notice and your 20-day enforcement clock depend on it.

3

Before you fund a reward or hire an investigator

Talk to a Georgia attorney and the detective first. A reward offer can be a binding promise, so the wording matters: who qualifies, whether it pays on arrest or conviction, and what happens if no one claims it. A private investigator should be licensed in Georgia and work with the detective, because interviewing witnesses first can hand the defense something to attack.

Parole, years later

The State Board of Pardons and Paroles must give you 20 days' advance notice when it considers granting parole, a release longer than 60 days, or a pardon, and let you file a written objection (O.C.G.A. 17-17-13). In a death penalty case, it must notify you within 72 hours of receiving a commutation request. None of this happens unless you have asked for notice and given the Board a current address and phone. Register with its Office of Victim Services. How to prepare for a parole hearing

06 Common questions

Questions about Georgia victims' rights

What rights do murder victims' families have in Georgia?

Under Marsy's Law, Georgia Constitution Article I, Section I, Paragraph XXX, and the Crime Victims' Bill of Rights, O.C.G.A. 17-17, a murder victim's family can get notice of court proceedings and of the accused's arrest or release, not be excluded from court, be heard at release, plea and sentencing, give the prosecutor their views on a plea, receive restitution, and object to parole.

Who counts as the victim when someone is killed in Georgia?

O.C.G.A. 17-17-3 names one relative in order of priority: the spouse; if none, an adult child; then a parent; then a sibling; then a grandparent. Other family members can still attend court and testify about the impact at sentencing under O.C.G.A. 17-10-1.2.

What do I do if my rights are ignored in Georgia?

File a motion in the criminal case as soon as possible and no later than 20 days after the denial, under O.C.G.A. 17-17-15. It applies when you asked in writing to be notified or heard and were not, or another provision was not followed. The motion is the only enforcement route, and the judge's ruling is final.

Can I speak at the sentencing in Georgia?

Yes. O.C.G.A. 17-10-1.2 requires the court to let the victim, the family, or others with personal knowledge testify about the crime's impact before sentencing. In Georgia that testimony is given in the defendant's presence and is subject to cross-examination, even when a written or recorded statement is allowed instead.

Does Georgia pay for a murder victim's funeral?

The Georgia Crime Victims Compensation Program, run by the Criminal Justice Coordinating Council, pays up to $6,000 for funeral expenses within a $25,000 total cap, plus up to $3,000 in counseling for each eligible family member. File within three years of the crime or the death. Call 877-231-6590.

How do I object to parole in Georgia?

Register with the State Board of Pardons and Paroles Office of Victim Services at 404-651-6668. Under O.C.G.A. 17-17-13, the Board must give a registered victim 20 days' notice before deciding to grant parole or a pardon, and the chance to file a written objection.

Is there a statute of limitations on murder in Georgia?

No. Under O.C.G.A. 17-3-1(a), a prosecution for murder may be commenced at any time. An unsolved homicide in Georgia does not expire.
