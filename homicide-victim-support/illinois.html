State guide

Your rights in Illinois

Families of murder victims in Illinois have constitutional rights under Article I, Section 8.1 and the Rights of Crime Victims and Witnesses Act: notice of court proceedings, being present on the same basis as the accused, communicating with the prosecution, being heard at release, plea and sentencing, and restitution. Two family members act as the victim's representatives, and they have standing to enforce these rights in court.

Article I, Section 8.1, as amended November 4, 2014

Illinois strengthened its victims' rights amendment in 2014 and wrote out, step by step, how a family enforces them: a written notice, a motion, a ruling on the record and an appeal. Here is what that means for a homicide victim's family, in plain words.

01

What you are entitled to

When an adult is killed, Illinois law names two representatives to act as the victim. They can be the spouse, a parent, a child, a sibling, or the representative of the estate (725 ILCS 120/3). Decide together who the two will be and tell the prosecutor in writing. Release, sentence-reduction and clemency boards can recognize more family members for their own proceedings.

  • 01 To be treated with fairness and respect for your dignity and privacy, free from harassment, intimidation and abuse Article I, Section 8.1(a)(1).
  • 02 To get a written explanation of your rights from the police within 48 hours of their first contact with you 725 ILCS 120/4(b). It must explain crime victim compensation and how to reach the Attorney General. Police will ask you to sign a sheet acknowledging you received it.
  • 03 To a free copy of the police report, on request, within five business days 725 ILCS 120/4(b-5). Ask in writing and keep the copy of your request.
  • 04 To timely notice of all court proceedings, and to communicate with the prosecution Section 8.1(a)(3) and (4). If the State's Attorney makes a plea offer or starts negotiating without having consulted you, the office must notify you within two business days and confer with you (725 ILCS 120/4.5).
  • 05 To be present at the trial and all other court proceedings on the same basis as the accused Section 8.1(a)(10). A party who wants you excluded because you will testify must file a written motion at least 60 days before trial, with specific reasons, and the court must explain any exclusion on the record (725 ILCS 120/4.5).
  • 06 To have an advocate and a support person of your choice with you at all court proceedings Section 8.1(a)(11). You also have the right to retain your own attorney, who may be present at any interview or interaction with the criminal justice system (725 ILCS 120/4(d)).
  • 07 To be heard at any proceeding on release, plea or sentencing, or where one of your rights is at issue Section 8.1(a)(5). At sentencing, 725 ILCS 120/6 lets you give an oral or written statement, or have someone read yours. You are not put under oath or cross-examined, and the court must consider it. This is your victim impact statement.
  • 08 To have your family's safety considered in release decisions and conditions, and to be reasonably protected from the accused Section 8.1(a)(8) and (9). Illinois no longer uses cash bail. Since September 18, 2023, an accused person is released before trial unless the State files a petition to deny release and the judge holds a detention hearing (725 ILCS 5/110-6.1). The State's Attorney must notify you of that hearing and tell you that you can ask for a protective order there. If you have safety concerns, tell the prosecutor before the first court date.
  • 09 To timely disposition of the case following the arrest of the accused Section 8.1(a)(7). If you object to a continuance, tell the prosecutor in writing before the hearing.
  • 10 To restitution, and to be notified of the conviction, sentence, imprisonment and release Section 8.1(a)(6) and (12). The State's Attorney must request restitution at sentencing and in any plea agreement if you ask for it (725 ILCS 120/4.5). Keep every receipt from the first week on.
02 When a right is ignored

Illinois spells out how enforcement works. Under Section 8.1(b), you have standing to assert your rights in any court with jurisdiction over the case, and the court must rule promptly, though you do not become a party. 725 ILCS 120/4.5(c-5) sets the procedure: the prosecutor asserts the right, and if the prosecutor declines, it must tell you in time for you or your attorney to file a motion yourselves. Courts waive the filing fees, must state the reasons for a denial on the record, and their decisions can be appealed to the appellate court.

1

File the written notice now

Illinois has a form for this. The Notice of Victim's Assertion of Rights, prepared by the Attorney General, lists the rights you want. The State's Attorney files it with the court, and the judge and prosecutor must check it at the start of any hearing where a right may be at issue. You can revise it at any time.

2

Move for enforcement on the record

If a right is ignored, ask the prosecutor to file a motion or raise it orally in open court. If the prosecutor declines, you or your attorney may do it. If the prosecutor's motion is denied without you being heard, you have 10 days to file your own. Note that taking up a substantive issue at a hearing labeled a status hearing is itself a violation of your rights under the statute.

3

Complain to the Attorney General

Since 2023, the Attorney General has received and investigated complaints that an employee or office of the State of Illinois violated a victim's rights, and can recommend corrective action. When a court finds a violation, it must order the responsible employee to complete the Attorney General's training. Use the complaint form within 60 days of learning of the violation and within one year of it.

What a court can and cannot do

When a court finds a right was violated, it must hear from you and the parties and order a remedy, such as an order requiring the right to be honored, a declaratory judgment, mandamus, or reopening a proceeding that already happened. It cannot vacate a conviction, order a new trial, or award damages.

03 Compensation in Illinois

The program

The Crime Victims Compensation Program is run by the Office of the Attorney General, and claims are decided through the Illinois Court of Claims under 740 ILCS 45. For a homicide it can pay funeral, burial, cremation and headstone costs, travel to recover and transport the body, counseling, crime scene cleanup, loss of support for dependents, and lost earnings for family members, including time off for funeral preparation, bereavement and court appearances.

File within five years of the crime, or within one year after someone is charged, whichever applies (740 ILCS 45/6.1). The Attorney General can accept a late claim for good cause. The crime must have been reported within 72 hours, which can also be extended.

Funeral and related costs are capped at $10,000 per victim, and the total for a crime on or after August 7, 2022 is $45,000 per victim, or $27,000 for a crime from August 7, 1998 up to that date (740 ILCS 45/2 and 10.1). Life insurance beyond the first $25,000 and some benefits reduce an award, so ask before you file.

Before you file

Gather the itemized funeral statement, the headstone invoice, counseling bills, proof of the victim's income if anyone depended on it, records of time you missed from work for court, and anything already paid by insurance or a fundraiser. Undisclosed payments are a common reason a claim is reduced. The State's Attorney's victim witness staff can help you apply.

File even if something is missing, and ask what can be supplemented afterward. A filed claim with gaps beats a complete claim filed past the deadline.

The first two weeks, including what to keep

A separate deadline: wrongful death. A civil lawsuit runs on its own clock, apart from compensation and the criminal case. In Illinois that is generally two years from the death. Against the person who caused it by violent intentional conduct, you have five years, or one year after the criminal case if they were charged, but a suit against a local government is due within one year. 04 If the case is unsolved

Illinois has no statute of limitations on murder. Under 720 ILCS 5/3-5(a), a prosecution for first or second degree murder can begin at any time, so the case does not expire.

1

Ask for status updates in writing

On your request, the investigating agency must tell you the status of the investigation until someone is arrested or the case is closed, unless the State's Attorney decides disclosure would unreasonably interfere (725 ILCS 120/4.5(a)). Put the request in writing and keep one named contact.

2

You must be told if a case reopens

When police reopen a closed case to resume investigating, the statute requires them to notify you, with the same exception for interference (4.5(a-5)). Keep your address current with the agency so that notice can reach you years from now.

3

Before you fund a reward or hire an investigator

Talk to an Illinois attorney and the detective first. A reward offer can be a binding promise, so the wording matters: who qualifies, whether it pays on arrest or conviction, and what happens if no one claims it. A private investigator should be licensed in Illinois and work with the detective, because interviewing witnesses first can hand the defense something to attack.

Parole in Illinois

Illinois ended discretionary parole for crimes committed after 1978, so the Prisoner Review Board mostly handles older cases, release conditions and clemency. One newer path matters for murder cases: a person who was under 21 at the time of a first degree murder and was sentenced on or after June 1, 2019 can get a parole review after serving 20 years, or after 40 years if sentenced to natural life (730 ILCS 5/5-4.5-115). 06 Common questions

Questions about Illinois victims' rights

What rights do murder victims' families have in Illinois?

Under Article I, Section 8.1 of the Illinois Constitution and the Rights of Crime Victims and Witnesses Act, a murder victim's representatives can get timely notice of court proceedings, communicate with the prosecution, be present on the same basis as the accused, have an advocate and support person present, be heard at release, plea and sentencing, receive restitution, and enforce these rights in court.

Who counts as the victim when someone is killed in Illinois?

Under 725 ILCS 120/3, when an adult victim has died, two representatives act as the victim. They may be the spouse, a parent, a child, a sibling, or the representative of the victim's estate. Families usually agree on the two and tell the State's Attorney in writing.

Can I speak at the sentencing in Illinois?

Yes. 725 ILCS 120/6 lets a victim give an oral or written statement at sentencing, or have someone read it aloud. The person speaking is not put under oath or cross-examined, and the court must consider the statement in deciding the sentence.

What do I do if my rights are ignored in Illinois?

Ask the State's Attorney to file a motion or raise it in open court. If the prosecutor declines, you or your attorney may file the motion. Filing fees are waived, the court must state its reasons for any denial on the record, and you may appeal. You can also file a complaint with the Attorney General within 60 days.

Does Illinois pay for a murder victim's funeral?

The Attorney General's Crime Victims Compensation Program can pay up to $10,000 per victim for funeral, burial, cremation, headstone and related travel costs, within a $45,000 total for crimes since August 7, 2022. File within five years of the crime or one year after charges. Call 1-800-228-3368.

Can I get a copy of the police report in Illinois?

725 ILCS 120/4(b-5) says that on a victim's request, the law enforcement agency must provide a free copy of the police report as soon as practicable and no later than five business days. Make the request in writing and keep a copy.

Is there a statute of limitations on murder in Illinois?

No. Under 720 ILCS 5/3-5(a), a prosecution for first or second degree murder may be started at any time. An unsolved homicide in Illinois does not expire.
