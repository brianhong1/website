State guide

Your rights in Oregon

Families of homicide victims in Oregon have constitutional rights under Article I, Sections 42 and 43, and ORS Chapter 147: to be told their rights, to attend critical stages, to be heard at sentencing and release hearings, to be consulted on plea deals, and to prompt restitution. Several apply only if you ask, so ask in writing.

Adopted November 1999, Section 42 amended 2008

Oregon put victims' rights in its constitution in 1999 and built out the enforcement process in 2009. Several of those rights attach only when you ask for them, and one deadline is measured in days.

01

What you are entitled to

These are rights, not courtesies. Some of them only operate once you ask, so ask in writing and keep the copy.

  • 01 To be informed of your Section 42 rights as soon as reasonably practicable Under ORS 147.417, law enforcement must tell you, and must include the time period for any right that depends on you making a request.
  • 02 To be present at critical stages held in open court, and told of them in advance on request Oregon's rule on excluding witnesses does not reach the victim (ORS 40.385). A defendant who objects to your presence at trial must file within 35 days of arraignment, and the court can grant it only if your presence would violate the constitution (ORS 147.522). Advance notice of each hearing depends on your request, so make it in writing.
  • 03 To be heard at the pretrial release hearing and at sentencing This is the right behind your victim impact statement.
  • 04 To be consulted about plea negotiations in a violent felony, on request Many homicide cases end in a plea rather than a trial, which makes this one of the most important requests you will make.
  • 05 To refuse an interview, deposition or other discovery request by the defense Section 42(1)(c). If a defense investigator calls, you can say no, or set your own conditions. Tell the prosecutor about any contact.
  • 06 To prompt restitution from the person convicted of causing the loss Documented losses are what the court can order repaid. Keep the receipts.
  • 07 To a copy of a transcript of proceedings held in open court If one was otherwise prepared. There is no right to have one prepared for you.
  • 08 To reasonable protection from the accused Section 43 is a separate constitutional provision, and it reaches pretrial release decisions.
  • 09 To information about the conviction, sentence and future release, on request Section 42(1)(b). This is the right behind custody and release notification, so register with the Department of Corrections. The parole hearing guide covers what comes later.
02 When a right is ignored

Oregon runs a claim process at ORS 147.500 to 147.550. A victim, or a prosecuting attorney at the victim's request, can assert a right and ask the court for a remedy.

1

Put it in writing, the same day

Email the prosecutor's office. Name the right, the date it was missed, and what you want done about it. A dated email is the record that matters months later.

2

Ask for it on the record

A right raised in a hallway is a conversation. A right raised in open court is a ruling, and a ruling is something an appellate court can look at.

3

If you want legal help

You do not need a lawyer to use these rights. You can assert them yourself, or ask the prosecutor to. If you want someone on your side, keep in mind the prosecutor represents the state, not you. Oregon has a nonprofit that represents families at no cost specifically to enforce these rights. It is listed below.

The 35 day clock

A motion seeking a determination on an issue that will affect the conduct of the trial must generally be filed within 35 days of arraignment, or of the defendant's entry of the initial plea, whichever comes sooner. Later filing is allowed only in limited circumstances. Ask a victims' rights attorney early rather than at week six.

03 Compensation in Oregon

The program

The Crime Victims' Compensation Program sits in the Oregon Department of Justice, Crime Victim and Survivor Services Division, under ORS 147.005 to 147.367. Compensable losses include funeral expenses and counseling for surviving family members.

File within one year of the injury that caused the death, though the Department of Justice can extend that for good cause, and the crime must have been reported to law enforcement (ORS 147.015). Awards are capped at $47,000 in total. In a death, the program can pay up to $5,000 for the funeral, with more at the department's discretion, up to $2,500 for cleaning, and up to $20,000 in loss of support to dependents (ORS 147.035). Confirm the current limits with the Department of Justice before you file, and ask which receipts they need.

Before you file

Gather the itemized funeral statement, counseling invoices, proof of lost income, and anything already paid by insurance or a fundraiser. Programs reimburse from itemized receipts, and an undisclosed payment is a common reason a claim is reduced or clawed back later.

File even if something is missing, and ask what can be supplemented afterward. A filed claim with gaps beats a complete claim filed past the deadline.

04 If the case is unsolved

Oregon has no statute of limitations on murder. Under ORS 131.125(1), a prosecution for aggravated murder or murder can begin at any time, so the case does not expire.

What has changed is where tips go. Crime Stoppers of Oregon, which had taken anonymous tips and paid rewards since 1983, dissolved and stopped taking tips on September 30, 2026. It asked the public to send information directly to the agency investigating the case.

1

Where tips go now

To the investigating police department or sheriff's office, through its tip line or online form, and to the assigned detective. Get the detective's name, direct number, email and the case number in writing. If that detective has retired or moved on, ask who owns the case now.

Replace the Crime Stoppers number on every poster, flyer and post with the agency's tip line.

2

Federal and missing-person channels

If the case crosses state lines or involves federal land, the FBI takes tips at tips.fbi.gov or 1-800-CALL-FBI. Ask the detective whether that fits. If your loved one is missing, confirm with the agency that the case is entered and current in NamUs.

The cold case guide
3

Before you fund a reward or hire an investigator

Talk to an Oregon attorney and the detective first. A reward offer can be a binding promise, so the wording matters: who qualifies, whether it pays on arrest or conviction, and what happens if no one claims it. Detectives hold back details to test tips, and a campaign can give them away. Point every tip to the detective, not a family phone or page. An investigator should be licensed in Oregon and work with the detective, because interviewing witnesses first can hand the defense something to attack.

Anonymity and rewards

An agency's tip line may not offer the anonymity Crime Stoppers did. Ask the agency how it handles anonymous tips, so that when you urge people to come forward you can describe the process honestly. I have not found an Oregon statute or state program that replaces the Crime Stoppers reward. If you hear of one, confirm it with your county district attorney's office.

06 Common questions

Questions about Oregon victims' rights

What rights do homicide victims' families have in Oregon?

Under Oregon Constitution Article I, Sections 42 and 43, and ORS Chapter 147, a homicide victim's family has the right to be told of its rights, to be present at critical stages, to be heard at sentencing and release hearings, to be consulted about plea negotiations in a violent felony, to information on request about the sentence and future release, to prompt restitution, to a copy of an existing transcript, and to reasonable protection from the accused.

Which Oregon victims' rights must I request?

Several Oregon rights depend on a request: being told in advance of critical stages of the proceeding, getting information about the conviction, sentence and future release, and being consulted about plea negotiations in a violent felony. Under ORS 147.417, law enforcement must tell you the time period for any right that depends on a request. Make each request in writing and keep a copy.

What is the 35-day deadline in Oregon?

Under ORS 147.522, a motion seeking a determination on an issue that will affect the conduct of the trial must generally be filed within 35 days of arraignment, or of the defendant's entry of the initial plea, whichever comes sooner. Later filing is allowed only in limited circumstances, so talk to a victims' rights attorney early.

Does Oregon compensate families of homicide victims?

Yes. The Crime Victims' Compensation Program in the Oregon Department of Justice, Crime Victim and Survivor Services Division, operates under ORS 147.005 to 147.367. Compensable losses include funeral expenses and counseling for surviving family members. Apply within one year of the injury that caused the death unless the Department of Justice extends it for good cause. Awards are capped at $47,000, with up to $5,000 for the funeral plus more at the department's discretion (ORS 147.015 and 147.035). Call the Department of Justice at 503-378-5348.

Who can help me enforce my rights in Oregon?

The prosecutor represents the state, not your family. The Oregon Crime Victims Law Center provides free legal representation to enforce victims' rights in court, at 503-208-8160. Your county district attorney's victim assistance program is the first call for everything else, and Oregon Department of Corrections victim services, at 1-888-749-8080, handles custody and release notification.

Can I speak at the sentencing in Oregon?

Yes. Article I, Section 42(1)(a) of the Oregon Constitution gives you the right to be heard at the pretrial release hearing and at sentencing. Being told in advance when those hearings happen depends on your request, so ask the district attorney's office in writing for notice, then prepare a victim impact statement.

Is Crime Stoppers of Oregon still taking tips?

No. Crime Stoppers of Oregon dissolved and stopped taking tips on September 30, 2026. Send tips directly to the agency investigating the case, or to the assigned detective. If a poster or post still lists the Crime Stoppers number, replace it with the investigating agency's tip line.

Is there a statute of limitations on murder in Oregon?

No. Under ORS 131.125(1), a prosecution for aggravated murder or murder may be started at any time. An unsolved homicide in Oregon does not expire, whatever happens to the tip lines around it.
