State guide

Your rights in Texas

Families of murder victims in Texas have rights under Article I, Section 30 of the Texas Constitution and Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 56A: notice of court proceedings, being present, conferring with the prosecutor, notice of plea deals, restitution, a victim impact statement, and a statement to the defendant after sentencing. Most apply only on request, so ask in writing.

Article I, Section 30, added November 7, 1989

Texas put victims' rights in its constitution in 1989, and Chapter 56A spells them out. Two things set Texas apart: who counts as a homicide victim's family, and when the family speaks in court. Here is what that means for you, in plain words.

01

What you are entitled to

Chapter 56A gives these rights to a "close relative of a deceased victim": a spouse at the time of death, a parent, or an adult brother, sister or child (Art. 56A.001). Most only operate on request, so ask the prosecutor in writing and keep the copy.

  • 01 To be treated with fairness and with respect for your dignity and privacy, and to be reasonably protected from the accused Article I, Section 30(a). These two apply without a request.
  • 02 To have the magistrate consider your family's safety when bail is set Art. 56A.051(a)(2). Tell the prosecutor before the bail hearing if you have safety concerns.
  • 03 On request, to be told about court proceedings at least five business days ahead, or otherwise as soon as reasonably practicable, and told as soon as possible if one is canceled or moved Art. 56A.051(a)(3), as amended in 2025 by Senate Bill 761. This includes appellate proceedings, and the appellate court must tell you its decision after it is entered but before it is made public.
  • 04 To be present at all public court proceedings, subject to the judge's approval Art. 56A.051(b) and Section 30(b)(2). A judge can keep a family member who will testify out of the courtroom if hearing other witnesses would materially affect that testimony. Ask the prosecutor early whether that applies to anyone in your family.
  • 05 On request, to confer with a representative of the prosecutor's office, and to be told the existence and terms of any plea bargain before it goes to the court Section 30(b)(3) and Art. 56A.453. You can object, but the decision remains the prosecutor's.
  • 06 To complete a victim impact statement and have it considered by the prosecutor and judge before sentencing or before a plea bargain is accepted Art. 56A.051(a)(12). The same statement goes to the parole board later. See how to write one.
  • 07 To a separate or secure waiting area before you testify Art. 56A.051(a)(8). Ask the victim assistance coordinator to arrange it before the trial date, not at the courthouse door.
  • 08 To restitution, and to information about the conviction, sentence, imprisonment and release of the accused Section 30(b)(4) and (5). Keep every receipt from the first week on.
  • 09 In a capital case, not to be contacted by a defense victim outreach specialist unless you consent in writing Art. 56A.051(a)(14). If the court has funded a defense victim outreach specialist, it must mail you a written explanation of what that is, and you can name a victim service provider to receive any contact for you.
  • 10 To take part in the parole process, and to be notified of parole proceedings and release Art. 56A.051(a)(7). Register with the Texas Department of Criminal Justice Victim Services Division.
02 When a right is ignored

Texas gives you standing to enforce these rights, but not to act as a party. Under Section 30(e) and Art. 56A.054, a victim or close relative "does not have standing to participate as a party in a criminal proceeding or to contest the disposition of any charge." Since 2023, Art. 56A.0531 says you may assert your rights orally or in writing, yourself or through an attorney. The state, through its prosecuting attorney, also has the right to enforce them (Section 30(d)).

1

Put it in writing, the same day

Email the prosecutor's victim assistance coordinator. Name the right and the Chapter 56A article, the date it was missed, and what you want done. Article 56A.0531 says a written assertion counts, and a dated email is the record that matters months later.

2

Ask the prosecutor to raise it

Because you cannot act as a party, the prosecutor is usually the one who puts a violation in front of the judge. Ask them to, in writing. If the answer is no, ask that the refusal be in writing too.

3

Know what it cannot undo

Under Art. 56A.053, a missed right is not a ground for the defendant's appeal, and officials are not liable for it. And because victims cannot contest the disposition of a charge, a missed right will not undo a plea after the fact. Assert your rights before the hearing, not after.

The statement after sentencing

Texas is unusual in when the family speaks in person. Under Article 42.03, Section 1(b), the court must let a close relative appear and give a statement to the court and to the defendant, about the offense, the defendant and the effect on the victim. That statement comes after punishment is assessed and the sentence is pronounced, so it does not change the sentence. You may not ask the defendant questions, and the court reporter does not transcribe it. The court may not limit the number of family members who speak unless more would unreasonably delay the proceeding. What the judge considers before sentencing is the written victim impact statement, so file that first.

03 Compensation in Texas

The program

The Crime Victims' Compensation Program is run by the Office of the Attorney General under Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 56B. For a homicide it can help with funeral and burial costs, family members' travel to the funeral, counseling, loss of support for the victim's dependents, bereavement leave, crime scene cleaning, and lost earnings and travel for attending court and post-conviction proceedings, after insurance and other sources have paid (Art. 56B.003).

File within three years of the crime (Art. 56B.052). The Attorney General may extend that for good cause. Total awards for one victim are capped at $50,000 (Art. 56B.106).

For crimes after July 14, 2016, the program pays up to $6,500 for funeral and burial costs, and transporting the deceased more than 50 miles one way does not count toward that limit, according to the Attorney General. Confirm current limits before you plan around them.

Before you file

Gather the itemized funeral statement, counseling invoices, proof of the victim's income if anyone depended on it, and anything already paid by insurance or a fundraiser. The program pays only what other sources do not, and an undisclosed payment is a common reason a claim is reduced or has to be repaid later. The prosecutor's victim assistance coordinator can help with the application.

File even if something is missing, and ask what can be supplemented afterward. A filed claim with gaps beats a complete claim filed past the deadline.

The first two weeks, including what to keep

A separate deadline: wrongful death. A civil lawsuit runs on its own clock, apart from compensation and the criminal case. In Texas that is two years from the death, and a claim against a governmental unit needs notice within six months, sometimes sooner under a city charter. Wrongful death deadlines by state

04 If the case is unsolved

Texas has no statute of limitations on murder. Under Code of Criminal Procedure Article 12.01(1)(A), an indictment for murder or manslaughter can be presented at any time, so the case does not expire.

1

Keep one named contact

Get the detective's name, direct number, email and the case number in writing. When that detective retires or moves units, ask who owns the case now. Many Texas agencies also have a crime victim liaison under Art. 56A.203. Ask for that person by title.

2

Ask about the Texas Rangers

The Texas Ranger Unsolved Crimes Investigation Program, created in 2001, gives local agencies a way to investigate unsolved murders with the Rangers' help, and the Rangers post selected cold cases publicly. Ask the detective whether your case has been referred or could be.

3

Before you fund a reward or hire an investigator

Talk to a Texas attorney and the detective first. A reward offer can be a binding promise, so the wording matters: who qualifies, whether it pays on arrest or conviction, and what happens if no one claims it. A private investigator should be licensed in Texas and work with the detective, because interviewing witnesses first can hand the defense something to attack.

Parole, years later

At a Texas parole review, any number of people can send written statements, but only one family member can appear in person before board members. Under Government Code 508.153, if more than one of you qualifies, you must choose a single representative together. Decide who that will be before the review is scheduled, and register with TDCJ Victim Services so you are notified. How to prepare for a parole hearing

06 Common questions

Questions about Texas victims' rights

What rights do murder victims' families have in Texas?

Under Article I, Section 30 of the Texas Constitution and Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 56A, a murder victim's close relatives can ask for notice of court proceedings, be present at public proceedings, confer with the prosecutor, learn the terms of any plea bargain, submit a victim impact statement, receive restitution, take part in parole, and speak to the defendant after sentencing.

Who counts as a close relative of a murder victim in Texas?

Article 56A.001 defines a close relative of a deceased victim as the spouse at the time of death, a parent, or an adult brother, sister or child. Grandparents and minor siblings are not on that list, so families often pick one listed relative to receive notices and pass them on.

Can I speak at the sentencing in Texas?

Yes, but in Texas the in-person statement comes after the sentence is pronounced. Under Article 42.03, a close relative may address the court and the defendant about the offense and its effect, without asking the defendant questions. The written victim impact statement is what the judge considers before sentencing, so file it early.

Can a victim's family stop a plea deal in Texas?

No. The prosecutor must tell you the existence and terms of a plea bargain, and the judge must consider your victim impact statement before accepting it, but Article 56A.054 says victims have no standing to participate as a party or to contest the disposition of a charge.

Does Texas pay for a murder victim's funeral?

The Attorney General's Crime Victims' Compensation Program pays up to $6,500 for funeral and burial costs for crimes after July 14, 2016, within a $50,000 total cap. Apply within three years of the crime under Article 56B.052. Call 800-983-9933 to confirm current limits.

Who can speak at a parole hearing in Texas?

Any number of people can send written statements to the parole board. Under Government Code 508.153, only one person may appear in person before board members, and if several close relatives qualify, they must choose that representative together. Register with TDCJ Victim Services at 800-848-4284 for notice.

Is there a statute of limitations on murder in Texas?

No. Code of Criminal Procedure Article 12.01(1)(A) sets no limitation for murder or manslaughter. An unsolved homicide in Texas does not expire.
