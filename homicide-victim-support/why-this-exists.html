Why this page exists

I am not a stranger to this.

Why I built this guide after my mother and brother were murdered in The Dalles, Oregon.

My name is Brian Hong. On February 25, 2009, my mother and my brother were murdered by our neighbor in The Dalles, Oregon. What follows is what I learned the hard way, written down so you do not have to learn it the same way.

Everything else on this site is procedure. This page is the reason it is here.

01

What happened

February 2009

My mother was Patty Hong. My brother was Randy Hong.

They were the most loving and caring people I have ever known. The time I should have had with them was stolen, and I am never going to get it back.

A friend from high school called me at six in the morning. We had not spoken in about five years. I knew something was wrong before he said anything, from the sound of his voice.

He was trembling and crying and scared. All he could tell me was that my father and I needed to get home, because something had happened to my mother and my brother.

The sheriff's department called my father a short time later and said the same thing. Come home. They would not tell us anything over the phone.

What surprised me most was that my friends did not call. I think they were trying to be considerate and did not want to overwhelm me. I spoke with family and relatives constantly in those first weeks, and with almost nobody else.

Nobody handed me a manual. There was no playbook for what I was supposed to do, what I needed to follow up on, or who I was supposed to ask. My father and I spoke with the district attorney and he gave us some details, but it was not much.

I felt lost through all of it. I was trying to navigate a system I did not understand while carrying grief that nobody can prepare you for.

02

What nobody told us

The gap

Three things we had to find out on our own. Each one is now on this site, so you do not have to.

That compensation exists, and that it runs on a clock

Nobody explained at the start that the state runs a crime victims' compensation program, or that it can help with funeral costs, counseling, crime scene cleanup and lost income. We found out because the victim advocate group at the courthouse handed us a pamphlet and told us the deadline. We filed in time.

That is the part that still sits with me. We got what we needed because one office did its job, not because the system is built to make sure families are told. Nobody checks whether that pamphlet reached you. If it had not reached us, we would have missed the deadline without ever knowing there was one.

So the first thing I tell anyone is this: ask for the victim advocate by name, at the police department and at the prosecutor's office. Do not wait for the pamphlet to find you. The first two weeks exists because of this.

That their belongings do not come back quickly

My mother's and my brother's cell phones were held as evidence. I cannot tell you today whether we ever got them back. I might be able to, if anyone had told me to write down what was taken and who had it.

Property held as evidence can take months or years to be released, and no one tells you that you may have to make a formal request once the case concludes, or who that request goes to. You are left assuming someone will call when it is ready. So keep a list of what was taken and who has it, from the first week. That is on the checklist because of what I cannot remember.

That the man who killed them would set the pace, and we would follow

There was no single moment. It was the slow realization, over years, that the person who did this had rights the system was built to protect, and that we had a pamphlet.

Our neighbor, the man who killed them, set the pace. The trial was delayed again and again on mental health grounds, and every delay moved our lives with it. We could not plan around anything. We were told when to appear, and we appeared, and nobody asked what the waiting was doing to us.

That is what I mean when I say the legal system is not built for victims. Not that the people in it were cruel to us. The structure itself is organized around the accused, who has an attorney from the first day and can set aside his own right to a speedy trial whenever it suits him. The family is what the process accommodates when it has room.

03

Why I built this

The reason

I am an advocate for victims' rights, and especially for the families of homicide victims. There is not much out there that prepares you for what you are about to walk through. Families need every resource they can get, and most of them have to go and find it themselves.

It is rare for someone who lost a loved one to homicide to turn what they went through into something written down for the next family: the documentation, the checklists, the roadmap they wish they had been handed. Most of what families learn the hard way stays with them. I wanted to put mine where other people could use it.

Building this gave me something useful to do with what happened. If one family reads these pages and understands that they are not alone, that somebody else has walked through the worst part of their life and is still standing, then the evenings were worth it. I want families of homicide victims to know that I am still around, that they can ask me questions, and that they have a shoulder to lean on.

Ohio and Oregon are written in depth because I have lived in both and know something about how they work. I live in Ohio now, and there is no shortage of families here who are going to need this.

Everything else is written for anyone, anywhere. The laws change from state to state, but the journey does not. The order of events, the waiting, the paperwork, the questions you do not know to ask, all of it is the same wherever you are. Some of it will apply to you and some of it will not. A broad map still beats no map at all, and that is what the checklists are.

It will feel like the sky is falling. There is life after this. I am not going to tell you it gets easy, but you are not the first person to walk this road, and you are not walking it alone.

That is the whole reason I put my name on this. Reach out if you want help finding something, or guidance, or just someone to talk to who already knows. It is a very lonely road to navigate by yourself.

04 What this site is, and what it is not

What it is

  • Plain-language information about how the process works and what you are entitled to ask for.
  • Written and maintained by one person, for free, with no ads.
  • Sourced to the actual statute wherever a right or deadline is named, so you can check it yourself.
  • Yours to print, copy, hand to a relative, or read at 3am.

What it is not

  • I am not an attorney, and nothing here is legal advice. Rights, deadlines and procedure differ by state.
  • I cannot take on your case, review your documents, or speak to anyone on your behalf.
  • I am not an investigator, and I cannot look into what happened to your person.
  • This is not a crisis line. If you are in crisis, call or text 988. If you are in danger, call 911.
