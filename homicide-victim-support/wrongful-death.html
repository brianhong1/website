Guide

Two cases, two clocks

A wrongful death lawsuit is a civil case your family brings, separate from the criminal case the state brings. It has its own deadline, usually two years from the date of death, and in most states that clock keeps running while the criminal case is still going. Claims against a government agency can have notice deadlines as short as six months.

In the first months, whatever the criminal case is doing

Most families assume the prosecutor is handling everything. The prosecutor is handling the crime. Nobody is watching the civil deadline unless you do, and a murder trial can easily take longer than two years to reach a verdict.

01

The criminal case and the civil case

Same death, two different cases, run by different people for different reasons.

Criminal prosecution Wrongful death lawsuit
Who brings itThe state, through the district attorney. Your family is a witness and a victim, not a party.Your family, usually through the estate's representative or the relatives state law names.
Who controls itThe prosecutor decides the charges and any plea deal, and can drop the case.Your family and your lawyer decide whether to file, settle or go to trial.
The goalPunishment and public safety.Money for the family's losses: lost income and support, funeral costs, loss of companionship.
The outcomePrison, probation or acquittal.A money judgment or a settlement. No one goes to prison.
Proof neededBeyond a reasonable doubt, the highest standard there is.More likely than not, a much lower bar.
Who can be held responsibleOnly the person or people who committed the crime.The killer, and sometimes others who share responsibility, such as a landlord, bar, security company, employer or government agency.
Cost to youNothing.Usually nothing up front. Most of these lawyers are paid a percentage only if there is a recovery.
DeadlineMurder usually has none.Short. Usually two years, sometimes six months for a notice to a government agency.
02 What this means for your family

Knowing the deadline is not the same as deciding to sue. It only keeps the choice open.

1

Neither case depends on the other

Because the civil standard of proof is lower, a family can win a civil case after an acquittal, or when no one was ever charged. O.J. Simpson was acquitted of murder and later found liable for wrongful death. A conviction usually makes the civil case much easier, but it is not required.

2

The criminal case does not stop the civil clock

In most states the civil deadline counts from the date of death and keeps running through arrest, trial and appeal. A few states give extra time in homicide cases, shown below, but those rules are narrow. Waiting for the verdict is how families lose the claim.

3

A judgment is not the same as money

Many defendants have nothing to collect. Where real money comes from is usually a third party with insurance. Some families sue the killer anyway, for accountability, or to stand first in line if the person ever profits, from a book or a settlement of their own.

4

Restitution is something else

A criminal judge can order the person convicted to repay specific losses, such as the funeral. That is part of the sentence, not a lawsuit, and you ask for it through the prosecutor or the victim advocate. Crime victim compensation is a third, separate source. How compensation and restitution work

03 Deadlines in the state guides

Wrongful death deadlines by state

The general deadline is the one most families need. The homicide column is the exception, and most of those exceptions reach only the person who committed the crime.

State General deadline In a homicide case Against a government agency
AlabamaNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (Ala. Code 6-5-410) No special rule. The clock keeps running while a criminal case is pending. The family can sue even if no one was charged, convicted or acquitted (6-5-410). City or town: sworn written statement to the city clerk within 6 months (11-47-23, 11-47-192). County: claim within 12 months (11-12-8). State: claim to the Board of Adjustment within 2 years of the death, unless the claim is first taken to state court (41-9-65).
ArizonaNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (A.R.S. 12-542) No special rule for the deadline. A family member convicted of, or pleading guilty or no contest to, murder or manslaughter of the person cannot share in the recovery (12-612). Written notice of claim to the state agency, city or county within 180 days, stating a specific amount you would settle for (12-821.01), and the lawsuit within 1 year (12-821).
California 2 years from the death (CCP 335.1) Against a person convicted of a felony for the death, you also have one year after judgment is pronounced, if that is later (CCP 340.3). Written claim within 6 months of the death (Gov. Code 911.2).
ColoradoNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (C.R.S. 13-80-102) 4 years against a driver who committed vehicular homicide and left the scene, even without a conviction (13-80-102). No other homicide rule, and no pause for the criminal case. Written notice within 182 days of discovering the injury (24-10-109). Count from the date of death to be safe.
Florida 2 years from the death (95.11(5)(e)) No deadline for a claim against the person who caused the death by murder or manslaughter (95.11(11)). Claims against businesses and others keep the 2 years. Written claim to the agency and the Department of Financial Services within 2 years (768.28(6)).
Georgia 2 years from the death (O.C.G.A. 9-3-33) No extension for the family's claim. A crime victim's own tort claims are paused while the prosecution is pending, for up to 6 years (O.C.G.A. 9-3-99), but the Court of Appeals has held that pause does not cover a family's wrongful death claim (Sachy v. Allen, 2022). Plan on 2 years from the death. Ante litem notice within 12 months for the state (O.C.G.A. 50-21-26) and 6 months for a city (O.C.G.A. 36-33-5). Counties have their own rule.
Illinois 2 years from the death (740 ILCS 180/2(d)) 5 years from the death if it resulted from violent intentional conduct, or 1 year after the criminal case ends if the person was charged with murder or another listed homicide. Applies only against that person (180/2(e)). Suit against a local government within 1 year (745 ILCS 10/8-101).
IndianaNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (Ind. Code 34-23-1-1) No special rule. The criminal case does not pause or extend the deadline. Written notice within 180 days to a city, county or other local government (34-13-3-8), or within 270 days to the state (34-13-3-6).
KentuckyNo state guide yet 1 year after an estate representative is appointed, or 2 years from the death if none is appointed in the first year (KRS 413.140(1)(a), 413.180) No special rule. The criminal case does not pause the clock, so have an estate representative appointed early. Claim against the state to the Board of Claims within 1 year (KRS 49.120).
LouisianaNo state guide yet 1 year from the death, or 2 years from the injury if longer (La. C.C. art. 2315.2(B)). In effect since Aug. 1, 2025; for an earlier death, do not assume the longer period applies. No separate homicide rule, and no pause for the criminal case. Since August 2025 every wrongful death claim gets at least 2 years from the injury. No separate notice step, but after filing suit you must request service on the government within 90 days or the claim against it is dismissed without prejudice (R.S. 13:5107(D)).
MarylandNo state guide yet 3 years from the death (Cts. & Jud. Proc. 3-904) If the person responsible or an accomplice concealed the killing, the 3 years can start when the homicide and the person responsible were, or should have been, discovered. Once a charging document is filed and made public, courts presume the person responsible should have been discovered (3-904). Written notice to a county or city within 1 year (Cts. & Jud. Proc. 5-304), or a written claim to the State Treasurer within 1 year (State Gov’t 12-106). A court can excuse late notice for good cause, and local notice is not needed if the government already knew of the injury within the year.
MichiganNo state guide yet 3 years from the death (MCL 600.5805) No special rule for homicide. If the person responsible hid the claim or their identity, you get 2 years after discovering it (MCL 600.5855). An estate representative appointed late gets 2 years from appointment, up to 3 years past the normal deadline (MCL 600.5852). Against the state, a signed, sworn claim or notice of intent in the Court of Claims within 6 months of the event for injury or death claims (MCL 600.6431). For a defective road, notice within 120 days (MCL 691.1404).
MississippiNo state guide yet Generally 3 years from the death (Miss. Code 15-1-49) Shorter: courts apply a 1-year limit when the death was caused by an intentional act such as murder (Thiroux v. Austin, Miss. 1999; 15-1-35). Do not wait for the criminal case. Written notice of claim to the government’s chief officer (the chancery clerk for a county, the city clerk for a city) at least 90 days before suing. Suit within 1 year of the act; filing the notice pauses the clock for 95 days (11-46-11).
MissouriNo state guide yet 3 years from the death (RSMo 537.100) No special rule. Time the defendant is outside Missouri and cannot be served there does not count toward the 3 years. No general notice-of-claim step. Cities of 100,000 or more require notice to the mayor within 90 days for street or sidewalk defects (RSMo 82.210).
New JerseyNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (N.J.S.A. 2A:31-3) No deadline if the death resulted from murder, aggravated manslaughter or manslaughter and the person responsible was convicted, found not guilty by reason of insanity, or adjudicated delinquent (2A:31-3). Notice of claim within 90 days of the death (N.J.S.A. 59:8-8). A judge may allow late notice up to 1 year after the death if the government was not seriously harmed by the delay and the family shows extraordinary circumstances (N.J.S.A. 59:8-9).
New YorkNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (EPTL 5-4.1) If a criminal case was started against the person responsible, you have at least 1 year after it ends (EPTL 5-4.1). Notice of claim to a city, county or other local government within 90 days of the estate representative’s appointment (Gen. Mun. Law 50-e). Suit against a city or county within 2 years of the death (Gen. Mun. Law 50-i). Against the state, a claim or notice of intention within 90 days of appointment, and the claim itself within 2 years of the death (Ct. Claims Act 10(2)).
North CarolinaNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (G.S. 1-53(4)) No special rule. The clock does not wait for the criminal case. Claims against the state go to the Industrial Commission within 2 years of the death (G.S. 143-299).
Ohio 2 years from the death (R.C. 2125.02(F)) None. The 2 years runs from the death even if no one has been charged. Ohio's rule that stops the clock while a defendant is in prison (R.C. 2305.15(B)) does not list the wrongful death statute. Generally 2 years against a political subdivision (R.C. 2744.04). Claims against the state go to the Court of Claims.
Oregon 3 years from discovery of the injury that caused death, and never later than 3 years after the death (ORS 30.020) None. However long the investigation takes, the outer limit is 3 years after the death (ORS 30.020(1)). Tort claim notice within 1 year (ORS 30.275(2)).
Pennsylvania 2 years from the death (42 Pa.C.S. 5524(2)) None. The 2 years runs from the death even if no one has been charged. Courts allow more time only in narrow cases, such as when the defendant actively hid the facts. Written notice within 6 months (42 Pa.C.S. 5522).
South CarolinaNo state guide yet 3 years from the death (S.C. Code 15-3-530(6)) No special rule. The claim is allowed even when the death was a felony (15-51-10), but the clock does not pause for the criminal case. A verified claim to the state or local government is optional, but if you file one it must arrive within 1 year (15-78-80). Suit within 2 years, or 3 if a claim was filed first (15-78-110).
TennesseeNo state guide yet 1 year from the injury that caused the death, not from the death itself (Tenn. Code 28-3-104(a)(1)) 2 years against a person whose criminal prosecution for the conduct began within the first year (28-3-104(a)(2)). It is not settled that this covers a family suing over a death, so plan to file within 1 year. Written notice to the Division of Claims and Risk Management for a claim against the state, within the same time limit as a lawsuit (9-8-402). Suit against a local government within 12 months (29-20-305).
Texas 2 years from the death (CPRC 16.003(b)) None. The 2 years runs from the death even if no one has been charged. Time the defendant spends outside Texas may not count (CPRC 16.063), but do not rely on that without an attorney. Notice within 6 months, or sooner if a city charter says so (CPRC 101.101).
VirginiaNo state guide yet 2 years from the death (Va. Code 8.01-244) The clock pauses from the first warrant, indictment or court appearance in a criminal case over the death until final judgment or appeal. Afterward you get the time that was left or 1 year, whichever is longer (8.01-229(K)). Written notice to the state within 1 year (8.01-195.7). For a negligence claim against a city, county or town, notice within 6 months unless the locality already knew of the claim (15.2-209).
WashingtonNo state guide yet 3 years from the death (RCW 4.16.080(2)) No special rule. The claim is allowed even if the death was a felony (RCW 4.20.010), but the deadline does not change. File a claim form with the state (RCW 4.92.110) or local government (RCW 4.96.020) before the 3 years run out, then wait 60 days before suing. The 60 days does not count against your deadline.

Why the last column matters. A claim against a government agency, such as a jail that released someone early or a city that ignored a known danger, often needs a written notice long before the lawsuit itself. These are the deadlines families miss most, because they can arrive before the first court date in the criminal case.

Not in one of these states? The general deadline is two or three years in most of the country, but a few states are shorter. Ask a civil attorney licensed where the death happened.

04 Talking to a civil attorney

Three questions to ask

  • What is my exact deadline, and what date does it count from?
  • Does any rule for criminal cases give us more time here, and against whom?
  • Could a government agency, landlord, business or employer share responsibility, and what notice deadline applies to them?

Write the date down where the rest of the family can find it, next to the case number.

What to expect

A first consultation is usually free. Most wrongful death attorneys work on contingency, taking a percentage of any recovery and nothing if there is none. Ask what the percentage is and who pays the case costs before you sign anything.

Filing a civil case can affect the criminal one. Evidence, depositions and timing can overlap. A good civil attorney will coordinate with the prosecutor rather than get in the way, and many will file to protect the deadline and then ask the court to pause the civil case until the trial is over.

A civil attorney is different from a victims' rights attorney, who helps you enforce your rights inside the criminal case. You may want both. Which rights apply to your case

05 Common questions

Questions about wrongful death claims

Can we file a wrongful death lawsuit if there was no conviction?

Yes. The civil case does not depend on the criminal one. It uses a lower standard of proof, more likely than not instead of beyond a reasonable doubt, so a family can win a civil case even after an acquittal, or when no one was ever charged.

Does the criminal case pause the wrongful death deadline?

In most states, no. The civil clock usually starts at the date of death and keeps running while the prosecution is pending. A few states give extra time in homicide cases, but those rules are narrow and often apply only to claims against the person who committed the crime. Ask a civil attorney for your exact date.

Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit?

It depends on the state. In some states the personal representative of the estate files on behalf of the family. In others, a spouse, children or parents file directly. A civil attorney can tell you who has the right to file in your state.

Does a wrongful death lawsuit cost money up front?

Usually not. Most wrongful death attorneys work on contingency, taking a percentage of any recovery and nothing if there is none. A first consultation is usually free. Ask about the percentage and who pays case costs before you sign anything.

Is restitution the same as a wrongful death lawsuit?

No. Restitution is ordered by the criminal judge as part of the sentence, to repay specific losses such as funeral costs. You ask for it through the prosecutor or victim advocate. A wrongful death lawsuit is a separate civil case the family brings, and it can cover losses restitution does not, such as the loss of companionship.
