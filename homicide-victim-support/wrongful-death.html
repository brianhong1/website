Guide

Two cases, two clocks

A wrongful death lawsuit is a civil case your family brings, separate from the criminal case the state brings. It has its own deadline, usually two years from the date of death, and in most states that clock keeps running while the criminal case is still going. Claims against a government agency can have notice deadlines as short as six months.

In the first months, whatever the criminal case is doing

Most families assume the prosecutor is handling everything. The prosecutor is handling the crime. Nobody is watching the civil deadline unless you do, and a murder trial can easily take longer than two years to reach a verdict.

The criminal case and the civil case

Same death, two different cases, run by different people for different reasons.

Criminal prosecution Wrongful death lawsuit
Who brings itThe state, through the district attorney. Your family is a witness and a victim, not a party.Your family, usually through the estate's representative or the relatives state law names.
Who controls itThe prosecutor decides the charges and any plea deal, and can drop the case.Your family and your lawyer decide whether to file, settle or go to trial.
The goalPunishment and public safety.Money for the family's losses: lost income and support, funeral costs, loss of companionship.

02 What this means for your family

Knowing the deadline is not the same as deciding to sue. It only keeps the choice open.

1

Neither case depends on the other

Because the civil standard of proof is lower, a family can win a civil case after an acquittal, or when no one was ever charged. O.J. Simpson was acquitted of murder and later found liable for wrongful death. A conviction usually makes the civil case much easier, but it is not required.

2

The criminal case does not stop the civil clock

In most states the civil deadline counts from the date of death and keeps running through arrest, trial and appeal. A few states give extra time in homicide cases, shown below, but those rules are narrow. Waiting for the verdict is how families lose the claim.

3

A judgment is not the same as money

Many defendants have nothing to collect. Where real money comes from is usually a third party with insurance. Some families sue the killer anyway, for accountability, or to stand first in line if the person ever profits, from a book or a settlement of their own.

4

Restitution is something else

A criminal judge can order the person convicted to repay specific losses, such as the funeral. That is part of the sentence, not a lawsuit, and you ask for it through the prosecutor or the victim advocate. Crime victim compensation is a third, separate source. How compensation and restitution work

03 Deadlines in the state guides

Wrongful death deadlines by state

The general deadline is the one most families need. The homicide column is the exception, and most of those exceptions reach only the person who committed the crime.

State General deadline In a homicide case Against a government agency
California 2 years from the death (CCP 335.1) Against a person convicted of a felony for the death, you also have one year after judgment is pronounced, if that is later (CCP 340.3). Written claim within 6 months of the death (Gov. Code 911.2).
Florida 2 years from the death (95.11(5)(e)) No deadline for a claim against the person who caused the death by murder or manslaughter (95.11(11)). Claims against businesses and others keep the 2 years. Written claim to the agency and the Department of Financial Services within 2 years (768.28(6)).
Georgia 2 years from the death (O.C.G.A. 9-3-33) For a crime victim's tort claims, the clock is paused from the crime until the prosecution is final, for up to 6 years (O.C.G.A. 9-3-99). Ask whether it covers your claim and every defendant. Ante litem notice within 12 months for the state (O.C.G.A. 50-21-26) and 6 months for a city (O.C.G.A. 36-33-5). Counties have their own rule.
Illinois 2 years from the death (740 ILCS 180/2(d)) 5 years from the death if it resulted from violent intentional conduct, or 1 year after the criminal case ends if the person was charged with murder or another listed homicide. Applies only against that person (180/2(e)). Suit against a local government within 1 year (745 ILCS 10/8-101).
Ohio 2 years from the death (R.C. 2125.02(F)) No homicide-specific extension found. Ask an attorney. Generally 2 years against a political subdivision (R.C. 2744.04). Claims against the state go to the Court of Claims.
Oregon 3 years from discovery of the injury that caused death (ORS 30.020) No homicide-specific extension found. Ask an attorney. Tort claim notice within 1 year (ORS 30.275(2)).
Pennsylvania 2 years from the death (42 Pa.C.S. 5524(2)) No homicide-specific extension found. Ask an attorney. Written notice within 6 months (42 Pa.C.S. 5522).
Texas 2 years from the death (CPRC 16.003(b)) No homicide-specific extension found. Ask an attorney. Notice within 6 months, or sooner if a city charter says so (CPRC 101.101).

Why the last column matters. A claim against a government agency, such as a jail that released someone early or a city that ignored a known danger, often needs a written notice long before the lawsuit itself. These are the deadlines families miss most, because they can arrive before the first court date in the criminal case.

Not in one of these states? The general deadline is two or three years in most of the country, but a few states are shorter. Ask a civil attorney licensed where the death happened.

04 Talking to a civil attorney

Three questions to ask

  • What is my exact deadline, and what date does it count from?
  • Does any rule for criminal cases give us more time here, and against whom?
  • Could a government agency, landlord, business or employer share responsibility, and what notice deadline applies to them?

Write the date down where the rest of the family can find it, next to the case number.

What to expect

A first consultation is usually free. Most wrongful death attorneys work on contingency, taking a percentage of any recovery and nothing if there is none. Ask what the percentage is and who pays the case costs before you sign anything.

Filing a civil case can affect the criminal one. Evidence, depositions and timing can overlap. A good civil attorney will coordinate with the prosecutor rather than get in the way, and many will file to protect the deadline and then ask the court to pause the civil case until the trial is over.

A civil attorney is different from a victims' rights attorney, who helps you enforce your rights inside the criminal case. You may want both. Which rights apply to your case

05 Common questions

Questions about wrongful death claims

Can we file a wrongful death lawsuit if there was no conviction?

Yes. The civil case does not depend on the criminal one. It uses a lower standard of proof, more likely than not instead of beyond a reasonable doubt, so a family can win a civil case even after an acquittal, or when no one was ever charged.

Does the criminal case pause the wrongful death deadline?

In most states, no. The civil clock usually starts at the date of death and keeps running while the prosecution is pending. A few states give extra time in homicide cases, but those rules are narrow and often apply only to claims against the person who committed the crime. Ask a civil attorney for your exact date.

Who can file a wrongful death lawsuit?

It depends on the state. In some states the personal representative of the estate files on behalf of the family. In others, a spouse, children or parents file directly. A civil attorney can tell you who has the right to file in your state.

Does a wrongful death lawsuit cost money up front?

Usually not. Most wrongful death attorneys work on contingency, taking a percentage of any recovery and nothing if there is none. A first consultation is usually free. Ask about the percentage and who pays case costs before you sign anything.

Is restitution the same as a wrongful death lawsuit?

No. Restitution is ordered by the criminal judge as part of the sentence, to repay specific losses such as funeral costs. You ask for it through the prosecutor or victim advocate. A wrongful death lawsuit is a separate civil case the family brings, and it can cover losses restitution does not, such as the loss of companionship.

California, Florida, Georgia
  • Cal. Code Civ. Proc. 340.3. One year after judgment for damages from a felony the defendant was convicted of.
  • Cal. Gov. Code 911.2. Six months to present a claim for death to a public entity.
  • Fla. Stat. 95.11(5)(e) and (11). Two years for wrongful death, and no limit against a person for a death by murder or manslaughter.
  • Fla. Stat. 768.28(6). Two years to present a wrongful death claim against the state or a subdivision.
  • O.C.G.A. 9-3-99. Tolling for a crime victim's tort claims while the prosecution is pending, up to six years.
Illinois, Ohio, Oregon
  • 740 ILCS 180/2. Two years, or five years for violent intentional conduct, or one year after the criminal case for listed homicide charges.
  • Ohio R.C. 2125.02(F). Two years after the death.
  • ORS 30.020. Three years after the injury causing death is discovered.
  • ORS 30.275. One year to give notice of a wrongful death claim against a public body.
Pennsylvania, Texas
  • 42 Pa.C.S. 5524(2). Two years for an action for death caused by wrongful act or negligence.
  • 42 Pa.C.S. 5522. Six months to give written notice to a government unit.
  • Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code 16.003(b). Two years, accruing on the death.
  • Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code 101.101. Six months to notify a governmental unit, unless it already has actual notice of the death.
